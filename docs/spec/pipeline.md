# Pipeline per search

1. API inserts a `searches` row (`queued`) and a `find` job.
2. Worker runs the source adapter, inserts `companies` rows (deduped per search by normalized domain), and enqueues one `crawl` job per company. It stops at the search's `limit` (default 100, at most the plan's search limit, see [Plans](plans.md)). A `domains` search has as many as its list: the API cuts the list to `limit` when one is given and stores the length as the limit. Its find job may run 30 minutes, since names are looked up at Brave's rate. An `icp` search's limit counts the companies fit keeps after the crawl: at the limit its find waits up to 2 minutes (`FIND_SETTLE_MS`) for the crawls and fit of its companies, and finds on in the place of those turned down (`dropped` in the adapter's context).
3. `crawl`: skip the company if its domain is suppressed (see [Privacy](privacy.md)). Otherwise check the shared cache (`domains`): a fresh row (`recheck_at` in the future) is reused when the last crawl found no personal addresses, or when it failed or was blocked. Reuse copies the domain's cached generic addresses (`domain_emails`) into this search's `emails`, minus suppressed ones, without fetching anything. In every other case crawl again: fetch the homepage (trying plain http and the www twin when the given address fails, and following a meta refresh or script redirect unless it leads to a platform) plus likely pages (contact, about, team, impressum, privacy, legal, in a dozen languages) from its links, from its sitemap when it links to fewer than two, and guessed paths (`/contact`, `/kontakt`, `/about`, `/impressum` and more) while no address on the company's own domain has turned up, max 8 pages (sitemap files count) and 2 MB per company. Respect robots.txt. Identify as `nvokebot/1.0 (+https://nvoke.run/bot)`. At most 1 request per second per domain and a global concurrency cap. Extract emails from text, `mailto:` links, Cloudflare email protection, JSON-LD, schema.org microdata and hCard, contact form recipients, vCards and the usual obfuscations ("name [at] domain [dot] com", "(a)", split attributes, reversed text). `docs/research/crawl-benchmark.md` measures every change to this step. Port and extend `../probe/apps/worker/src/contact/extract.ts`. Drop image filenames, placeholder addresses (example.com, sentry, wixpress, etc.) and addresses on unrelated domains unless they are the only ones found. Classify each address `generic` (role mailbox) or `personal` (always for webmail such as gmail.com). Then drop suppressed addresses, store all remaining addresses in this search's `emails`, upsert the `domains` row (`recheck_at` 90 days after a crawl, 7 days after a failed or blocked one, `has_personal` set when personal addresses were found), upsert the generic addresses into `domain_emails` (`first_seen` never changes, `last_seen` moves to now) and delete the domain's cached addresses this crawl no longer found. It also replaces the domain's kept page text (`domain_pages`, for openers): the homepage and up to two more pages it read, skipping contact, team and legal pages, 3,000 characters of visible text each, with every email address taken out. Personal addresses never go into the shared cache. The crawl ends in one of five outcomes: `done`, `failed` (unreachable, or an error answer), `blocked` (robots.txt or a suppression keeps us out), `refused` (the homepage answered 401, 403, 429 or 503, or served a bot challenge such as "Just a moment..." or a CAPTCHA: the site refuses our servers) and `js_only` (the crawl finished without an address and the homepage is an app shell: under 200 characters of visible text with scripts, or a `noscript` that asks for JavaScript). `refused` and `js_only` are cached for 7 days like a failure. The desktop app reads those, and `done` sites without an address, from the user's computer (see [Local render crawl](local-render-crawl.md)). An `icp` company (`companies.fit_state = 'gate'`) whose crawl found an address that could ever be checked (not off target, and a role inbox only when it could be the fallback or the search checks inboxes) or a named person who is not off target (`needsFit`), or people pages for Haiku to read, gets no check and no people job yet: it waits for fit's second look (`fit_state = 'pending'`, see [Describe your customer](sonar.md)). Addresses the local render crawl hands in for such a company wait the same way. Before that the worker looks up the domain's MX for free: a domain that takes no mail never waits for fit for its own addresses (they go to their checks and settle invalid at no charge) and keeps no people.
4. `verify`: per email, check syntax, common freemail typos (`gmial.com` and the like, `invalid`), the disposable domain list and MX records (a domain whose MX belongs to a parking service is `invalid`), then an SMTP RCPT probe without sending (EHLO, MAIL FROM, RCPT TO, QUIT). The same session asks about two random addresses on the domain; if the server accepts either, the domain is `catch_all`. A 4xx reply (greylisting), a mail server we cannot connect to or that stalls mid session are not cached: the verify job is queued again 10 minutes later, twice, before the address settles as `unknown`. A check we could not make on our side is not a retry and never settles for that reason: every lane at its budget (`smtp_paced`), port 25 blocked on every IPv4 lane, Google or Microsoft refusing or stalling our connection (`smtp_throttled`, they do that when one IP probes many of their domains), or the job running out of time while it waited for the mail server. It goes back in the queue in the same job row, after 1 minute when only the slots were taken, else 10 minutes, doubling each time up to 2 hours, with up to 20% jitter, and settles `unknown` (reason `capacity_deadline`) only 48 hours after its first such wait. Such a check is pending like any other: the search keeps running and `queuedChecks` (`src/worker/queue.ts`) counts it for the escalation logic. Sessions go out over three kinds of lane, in order: IPv6 when the MX host has an AAAA record (Google's do, Microsoft's `mail.protection.outlook.com` mostly do not), then this VPS's IPv4, then each relay's IPv4 (`SMTP_RELAYS`). Hostinger allows 5 port 25 connections a minute per VPS IPv4 address, so each IPv4 lane opens at most `SMTP_PROBES_PER_MINUTE` a minute (this VPS gives the free tools their share); IPv6 is not throttled. The per provider limit is shared by all lanes, so no provider sees more than one well behaved sender. A lane counts as blocked only when its connections fail at 5 different MX providers in a row and canary connections over that lane to Gmail's, iCloud's and Yahoo's MX fail too. That lane alone then pauses for 10 minutes and the operator gets an alert naming it (`smtp-port25-blocked`, at most every 6 hours); a relay whose tunnel is down is left alone for a minute. Every `unknown` result and every requeue is logged with its reason and search id. The people job follows the same rule: a probe we could not make on our side goes back in the same job row with the same backoff, never as its one retry, and after 48 hours of such waits the job ends with those people left without an address; a real server answer keeps its one retry. Cache results per address for 30 days (the retention of `email_checks`), `unknown` for 1 day. A charged address was confirmed within the month; the owner chose fewer repeat probes over a weekly recheck (docs/research/cost.md). A `valid` result is charged against the user's plan in the same transaction (see [Plans](plans.md)); every other result is free.
   Cached addresses go through the same verify step and count toward the quota the same way: a user pays per valid verification, wherever the address came from.
   Checks go to named people (`checkPolicy` in `src/leads/check-policy.ts`, applied by the verify job and once more when a result is recorded). A named person with a title or a seniority who is not off target is checked at once. Every other address waits only for those, so no waiting address waits for another: an unnamed personal address (no first and last name: max@, mh@) waits until the company's named people settle and is left unchecked (`emails.skipped = 'unnamed'`) once any named person is valid. A named person with no title and no seniority (most often a name read from the address) waits until the titled named people settle and is left out (`skipped = 'untitled'`) once one of the company's decision makers or sales, growth, marketing, business development or partnership people is valid; with none of those valid it is checked. People in functions a seller does not write to are never checked (`skipped = 'off_target'`), whatever else the company has: HR, recruiting, data protection, customer support and success, bookkeeping and finance, office staff, assistants and interns in any team (an assistant to the CEO or the Geschäftsführung too), and C level or VPs in finance, people or customer success (CFO, CHRO, Chief People Officer, VP Customer Success) unless the title is commercial (`isOffTargetTitle` in `@nvoke/shared/crawl`). A founder is never off target. Role inboxes (`kind = 'generic'`) are not checked by default, with one exception: a company with no valid personal address gets one fallback inbox checked. It is the best of its role inboxes by `inboxRank` (sales@ first, then hello@, contact@, info@, office@, then unlisted ones, then support@, billing@, jobs@ and press@, privacy@ and legal@ last, no-reply and postmaster never; ties go to the first address). It waits until every personal address that is not off target settles, so a named person is always preferred, and it is checked only when none of them is valid. Only one per company is checked: once one has a result, or while a better one exists, the rest stay unchecked, hidden and free (`skipped = 'role_inbox'`). A valid fallback inbox is charged like any verified email and shown like any other result. The search can ask for all of its role inboxes (`searches.check_inboxes`, default false, set by `checkInboxes` on create or on `PATCH /v1/searches/:id`, where switching it on clears `skipped` on the search's role inboxes skipped for that reason and queues their checks at priority -10, after every named person). With it on, every role inbox waits for the named people and is left out once any personal address is valid. A waiting check goes back in the queue marked `deferred` and is woken the moment a check of its company settles or its people job ends (5 minutes at the latest); after 12 defers or 30 minutes it is decided without waiting more. An address that became unnecessary while it was being checked is recorded skipped, not charged. A skipped address counts as settled for `lead.ready` and for finishing the search, and Search more does not queue it again.
   A personal address nobody on the site was printed for still names its owner when its local part is first.last, first_last or first-last and both parts read as a name (the first a common first name or one the site printed): `first_name` and `last_name` are filled, `title` stays empty and `confidence` is 80 instead of 90. A lone first name (max@) or a double first name (anne-marie@, karl-heinz@) stays unnamed unless a printed person matches it, and a name read this way never teaches the people job a domain's pattern. A "person" that is the company itself is dropped when the name has no first name in it (Agence Nova on agencenova.com) or is the company's whole name with no title; a titled founder the company is named after (Peter Jensen, Founder, at Peter Jensen ApS) is kept.
   The people step asks Haiku only when the rules leave names unread. The crawl reads people from schema.org (JSON-LD, microdata, RDFa), team card markup (a role field and the one name in its card), imprint labels and name and title lines, with titles in English, the Nordic languages, German, Dutch, French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Polish and Finnish. When it finds no decision maker, `needsPeopleLlm` checks the people pages for names it did not read. One name next to a founding verb or a decision maker's title is enough; otherwise there must be at least two, each a common first name or with a title word around it. Haiku then reads only the lines around those names (`peopleExcerpt`, at most 3,000 characters across pages, "..." where lines are left out), without reasoning, with at most 20 people and 1,500 output tokens. Every name it gives must still be printed on the full page, with its title printed near it. It reads during the crawl, except for an `icp` company: that one keeps the pages (`companies.people_texts`, migration `1800_people_after_fit`) and Haiku reads them only once fit keeps the company, before its people job is queued; addresses stored before the read are linked to the people it names. A domain with no mail server (no MX and no address, or a null MX) gets no Claude read and no people job.
5. When all jobs for a search are finished, the search becomes `done` (or `failed` with an error).
6. A search grows as a list: once it ended (`done`, `failed` or `canceled`), `POST /v1/searches/:id/more` runs it again with new params of the same kind (a wider area, another keyword, more items). The new params become the search's, its `limit` becomes the companies it holds plus the new limit, the run is appended to `runs` and a find job goes in the queue. The unique `(search_id, domain)` keeps companies it already holds out, so a run only adds what is new; counters keep counting. Webhooks send `lead.ready` for the new leads; `search.finished` goes out once per search, after the first run that ends `done` or `failed`. The new run also queues the crawls and checks a cancel or quota stop left undone, and waits (409 `search_running`) while a canceled run's find job is still stopping. Each run's find asks its source for the search's whole limit, since companies the search already holds are skipped.

Everything is a row in a Postgres `jobs` table claimed with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`. No Redis. Retries use exponential backoff, max 3 attempts. The worker handles SIGTERM cleanly. The worker also runs the retention purge (`apps/server/src/leads/retention.ts`) at start and then hourly (see [Privacy](privacy.md)).
