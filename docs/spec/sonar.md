# Sonar (Describe your customer)

Sonar is the product name of the `icp` kind (`ICP_NAME` in `plans.ts`) and nvoke's premium search. Both apps give it the display strip's black material, a lit LED and a ×3 badge wherever it appears, so it reads as the one black key on the panel.


Kind `icp` (`apps/server/src/leads/sources/icp.ts`). The user says who they sell to in plain words; nvoke works out where those companies are, keeps pushing until the search holds `limit` companies and, past that, until it holds enough verified emails, and says what it does in `searches.stage` and `stage_note` (shown as the live progress line). A search that comes up short never just ends.

- **Plan** (`src/leads/planner.ts`). One Sonnet call (`quality`, task `plan`) turns the words into a plan stored in `searches.plan`: core queries phrased the way such companies describe themselves on their own sites (10 to 16, in the country's language where its companies use it), list page and directory queries (6 to 14, partner and agency directories of the tools such companies use among them), adjacent niches and synonyms (8 to 14), wider geography (8 to 14: more countries when the user named none, else more cities), trade and city pairs when the customer is a local business, sales job titles only when the description or the plan's segments name sales or growth teams (sales teams, SDRs, BDRs, account executives, heads of sales or growth, RevOps, outbound teams; founders or B2B companies alone are not enough), up to 4, the usual sales development, account executive and business development titles when Claude names none, fit criteria and disqualifiers. The job titles become job post queries (see "Job posts"), one per title and country, at most 8. Every query has a country. Countries the user named are never left. A retry or a later run with the same words reuses the stored plan.
- **Company index** (`src/leads/company-index.ts`, migration `1720_company_index`). Before each paid tier (and so before any web search), the run asks nvoke's own index: the kept page text of every crawled domain (`domain_pages`, up to 3 pages, 12 months) and the launches in `feed_items`, each with a full text vector (`simple` and `english`, so words match in any language and English plurals match too) and a GIN index. A trigger fills the vector on every insert and text change; the rows kept before it are filled in batches of 500 by `1721_company_index_backfill`, which runs outside a transaction (a file whose first line is `-- migrate: no transaction` runs statement by statement) and builds the indexes concurrently, so neither live table is rewritten or locked for long. Anything shaped like an email address is cut out before indexing, so the index holds company data only. The words of every core, adjacent and wider query of the plan, and of each segment, are asked once each, and any of them may match: a page need not hold every word of a query, but it must hold at least 2 of the plan's words (`MIN_MATCHED`, all of them when the plan has fewer). Companies come back by how many plan words their best page holds, then by text rank, one per site, at most 3 per wanted company (300 at most), without the sites the search holds or turned down, suppressed domains, domains whose last crawl failed, was blocked or refused us, and domains whose country TLD is outside the plan's countries (generic TLDs pass). They get the same first look as web results, with the kept homepage's title and description in it. The note says "Checking N companies nvoke already knows". Every later ask leaves out every site the run has seen by then, so it brings only new ones; once an ask comes back with fewer than it asked for, the run asks no more. Web search pays only for what the index left short. A failing index is logged and the search goes on. Companies from it have source `index`.
- **Tiers.** Tier 1 runs the list page and directory queries first, then the core queries and the local pairs (OpenStreetMap only, with no Brave place lookups or web fallback, at most 60 companies a pair; a place the map cannot find is skipped with a note), interleaved and spread over countries so results arrive within seconds. Tier 2 widens to adjacent niches, tier 3 to wider geography, then the job post queries run, tier 4 reads result pages 2 to 4 only of queries whose last page kept at least 3 candidates, 40% of what it gave and twice the run's average of fits per page. Companies on a list page count toward the query that found the list. Job post queries run only when the plan wants them (see Plan); a stored plan's job posts are skipped otherwise. Each tier starts only while the search is short. When every tier is spent below the limit the stage is `exhausted` and the note says so plainly. Four failed queries in a row fail the find job, so it retries instead of reporting an empty plan as done. A canceled search stops at once and gets no more notes. Notes count candidates that passed the first look and were handed to the find job, which may still skip a company the search holds, and tier 4 counts those too; fit may still turn one down after the crawl.
- **List pages** (`src/leads/listicle.ts`). A result titled like a list ("Top 25 ... agencies", "The 10 best ...") or a directory category page (clutch.co, designrush, goodfirms, sortlist, agencyspotter, themanifest, upcity and a few more) is fetched with the crawler's rules and a fetch that connects only to public addresses over http(s), checked on every hop and for robots.txt (no private, loopback, link-local or metadata targets): robots.txt on every hop, one request per second per host, reading stops at 500 KB, no CAPTCHA solving, no logins. Pages are read 6 at a time, one per host, the next starting as soon as one finishes. The companies it links to (off its own site, not platforms, directories, trackers, shorteners or affiliate links, one per domain, navigation and footer skipped, directory redirects unwrapped) become candidates with the text next to the link. A list that named companies and links its next page plainly (`?page=2`, `?p=2`, `/page/2/`, on the same site with nothing else changed) is read up to 3 pages further. Titles like "B2B SaaS startups in Berlin" and "12 Belkins alternatives" count as lists too, and so do pages under `/partners`, `/agencies`, `/experts`, `/alternatives` or `/competitors` on any site (a tool's partner directory names the agencies that use it). At most 400 list pages a run, next pages included, and at most 100 MB and 12 minutes of list page reading; a host that refuses us or links to nothing but itself is not tried again in that run. Directory pages are never candidates themselves.
- **Job posts** (`src/leads/hiring.ts`). A company advertising a sales development, business development or account executive role (or a head of sales) is building an outbound team now. They run after the wider geography: a request names 15 to 20 companies, but most are larger software companies and fewer than a fifth fit. Job post queries look for a title on the public boards of the applicant tracking systems a country's companies use, in one Brave request: `"account executive" (site:jobs.lever.co OR site:job-boards.greenhouse.io OR site:boards.greenhouse.io OR site:jobs.ashbyhq.com OR site:apply.workable.com)`, with Personio first in Germany, Austria and Switzerland, Recruitee in the Netherlands and Belgium and Teamtailor in the Nordics. Any result on such a board whose title names a sales role (from any query) is mapped to the company's own site from its public board, once per board per run: Ashby's page data (`publicWebsite`), Workable's account (`apply.workable.com/api/v1/accounts/<name>`), Greenhouse's boards API (postings hosted on the company's careers page), else the board page's link to the company (a logo, home page or imprint link, the name matching the board), else a guess from the board's name (`.com`, `.io`, `.ai`) kept only when that homepage links back to the board, or when the name has at least 6 letters, is exactly the domain's label and the homepage title names it. A guessed site is marked in the fit line and caps the job post's weight at 74. A careers subdomain stands for the company's site. Fetched with the list page rules (robots.txt on every hop, the per host rate, no logins, no CAPTCHA solving), 8 boards at a time. The candidate has source `hiring`, the posting as `sourceRef`, "Hiring: <role>" as its title and a line for fit scoring with the role, the other open sales roles and the number of open roles on the board (60 or more reads as a large company). A board that does not say and a guess that is not confirmed drop the posting.
- **Fit** (`src/leads/fit.ts`, migration `1770_fit_after_crawl`). Platforms, webmail, directories, job boards and applicant tracking system hosts, review and data sites, public bodies and `jobs.`, `news.` or `blog.` hosts are dropped before any call. Haiku (`bulk`, task `fit`) scores 0 to 100 against the description, the product and the plan's criteria, twice at most. The first look reads what the search found for free: the name, the address and the search result's title and snippet (or the list page's line, or the homepage title and description the index kept), 100 to a call, with no reasons, and drops only what scores below 40 (`GATE_FLOOR`, "not a fit"); no homepage is read before the crawl. The rest is crawled. A company whose crawl found someone to check (`needsFit`, see [pipeline](pipeline.md) step 3) waits for the fit job: crawled companies are gathered per search and scored 30 to a call (`FIT_BATCH`) once 30 wait, 75 seconds after the first (`queueFit`, `FIT_WAIT_MS`), or as soon as no company of the search waits for its crawl (`flushFit`), from the homepage title, description and text the crawl kept (`domain_pages`) and how the search found it (a job post for a sales role, a list page). At 60 or more (`FIT_THRESHOLD`) it is kept with `fit` and `fit_reason`, and its checks and people job are queued. Below, it is deleted with its addresses and people before anything was checked or charged, its site joins the plan's turned down sites, and the search's counts drop back. A check that runs while its company waits is dropped and queued again when the company is kept. A company with nobody to check costs no second call and keeps the first look's score. For customers named by a role (founders of B2B companies, small sales teams, SDRs) a small B2B company needs outbound evidence to fit: a current job post for a sales role scores 75 to 90 when the company is not large, other evidence (a book a demo homepage with a sales team, an outbound tool it names) 60 to 79, and a B2B company with none 45 to 59. Agencies, freelancers and consultancies are judged by what they do. The candidates sit in a delimited block the model is told to treat as untrusted data. Verdicts are matched by domain: the first answer per asked domain wins, others are dropped. The search's brief (description, product, segments, criteria, disqualifiers) is sent as a cached prefix after the system prompt, so every call of a search and look reads it from the prompt cache. A failed call, or one that scores nobody, is tried again and split down to single candidates; candidates an answer skipped are asked again on their own, the rest kept; what still has no verdict scores 0 and is dropped, without joining the turned down sites. When Claude cannot be asked at all (no key, budget spent) Brave results go on unscored, list page links are left out, crawled companies are kept unscored, and the note says so; a fit job that runs out of attempts keeps its companies unscored too.
- **Search budget.** One run spends at most `ICP_BRAVE_BUDGET` paid web search credits (default 120; cache hits do not count). A credit is a request the provider billed, whichever provider answered: a Brave page is one, a Serper page of 20 results two. The ongoing budgets (`ICP_DAILY_BRAVE_BUDGET`, `ONGOING_DAILY_BRAVE_BUDGET`) count credits too, as does `searches.source_requests`. A page may take the run one credit past its budget. Past it the note says so, no paid tier starts, and the list pages and places already found are finished.
- **Allowance cap.** A run never pays for candidates the buyer cannot be billed for. Before the index, before each paid tier and after every 10 credits inside one (`QUOTA_RECHECK_CREDITS`), it reads what the account's allowance still takes for this search (`searchQuota` in `quota.ts`: what is left this month over `ICP_EMAIL_WEIGHT`) and how many valid addresses its settled companies gave (crawled and checked, or with nothing to check). Once the companies found and not yet settled, at that rate (0.1 a company until 30 have settled, `DEFAULT_VALID_PER_COMPANY`, never below 0.02), are expected to fill it, the run stops: no web search, no place on the map, no first look. The note says "The companies found can fill the N verified emails this month’s allowance still takes, so nvoke pays for no more searches" (or that the allowance is used up). A failing read is logged and the run goes on.
- **Never short** (`src/leads/ongoing.ts`). A search aims for verified emails, not companies: 30% of the limit the user asked for, with no floor (`VERIFIED_TARGET`; verified means `emails.verification = 'valid'` for the search), never more than the account's allowance can still take: the verified count plus what is left of this month's allowance, each email of the search counting as `ICP_EMAIL_WEIGHT` (`allowanceRoom`). With room for none, no round starts and the search ends with the allowance note. When its last job finishes short of that, `maybeFinishSearch` holds it `running` with stage `escalate` instead of ending it; the worker also holds it early once its find, crawls, fit and checks are done (no verify job queued, running or deferred, no company waiting for fit) and what is left cannot close the gap: the verified count plus the unchecked addresses at the share of checks that came back valid so far (every unchecked address while fewer than 20 came back), plus one per people job still queued or company with named people waiting for fit (`projectVerified`). The addresses of a company waiting for fit count as checks to come (`queuedChecks` in `src/worker/queue.ts`, at least one per such company). Addresses the policy will leave unchecked (role inboxes with inbox checks off, off-target people, and unnamed and untitled addresses of a company that already has what they wait for) do not count. So the next round starts within a tick or two and does not wait for paced people probes. No round starts while the search has checks queued or deferred; a held search that has some when claimed goes back to running ("Checking N addresses before looking further") and is judged again when its last job ends. Every 15 seconds the worker (and `cli search` and `pnpm eval`, which run the same loop) claims held searches (stage `replan`, with a claim time and attempt count in the plan, so a round a stopped worker or an unexpected error dropped is claimed again after 10, 20, then 40 minutes, and after 3 attempts the search ends saying planning failed) and asks the planner for a fresh round (`replan` in `planner.ts`, Sonnet, task `replan`): new adjacent niches, countries the search has not used (only when the user named none) or new cities in its countries, more list page and directory queries (two at each turn of the round's query budget), and new local pairs, given every query earlier plans ran (kept in `plan.ran`, at most 800), so nothing repeats. The round is stored as the plan (same key, `round` counts up) and runs as another run into the same list: `trigger: 'escalate'`, the user's latest limit as new companies, a find job, and the crawls and checks a quota stop dropped are queued again. Its note says what it does with counts from the database ("Found 140 verified so far. Looking in 9 more countries, 6 new niches and 4 more list page searches"). At most 3 fresh rounds in 24 hours per search (`MAX_ESCALATIONS_PER_DAY`), each with half of `ICP_BRAVE_BUDGET` (`FOLLOW_UP_BUDGET_SHARE`, at least 4 credits), carried in `plan.braveBudget`. A fresh round starts only when the run before it found at least 0.2 verified emails per credit it paid (`MIN_VALID_PER_CREDIT`): the icp adapter keeps the credits on the plan (`plan.paid`, `{ run, credits }`, `run` the number of run records, so a retried find adds to its round and a new round starts at zero), and the round's verified count is what was verified since it started plus, when judged early, what its open checks may still bring. A run that paid nothing is not judged by it. Below it the search ends `exhausted` ("The last round found 3 for 40 paid web searches, too few to pay for another"). Cancel and failure write an honest last note too ("Stopped", "Stopped by an error", "This month’s allowance is used up") instead of the step the search was on. A search ends `done` with a plain final note when it reaches the target, used its 3 rounds in the last 24 hours, found too few per credit in its last round, the account's allowance is used up, Claude is out of reach, or the planner has nothing new ("nvoke has run every search it could think of for this customer. Describe your customer more broadly or add countries to find more").
- **Ongoing.** An `icp` search with `ongoing` on runs one fresh round a day (`trigger: 'ongoing'`) at the time of day (UTC) it was created, when it ended before that time came round, so rounds do not drift later and new leads arrive over days. A daily round does not chain further rounds and its final note says what it added ("Added 12 verified emails in this daily round, 152 in all. Next round tomorrow"). Each daily round may spend `ICP_DAILY_BRAVE_BUDGET` paid web search credits (default 40) and all daily rounds together `ONGOING_DAILY_BRAVE_BUDGET` a UTC day (default 250). The round reserves its budget in `ongoing_budget` (one row per UTC day, an atomic add under the cap) before Claude plans it, gives it back when it does not start, and counts against the day it was reserved on even when it runs past midnight. The plan carries the reservation (`plan.braveBudget`) and the icp adapter holds the run to it. Searches waiting longest go first; the rest wait for the next day; a round under 4 credits is not started. Ended searches of any status are picked up (done, failed, or canceled and switched on again); a cancel switches ongoing off. While the account's allowance is used up the search stays ended with stage `paused` ("Paused until your allowance renews") and resumes when it renews. Switching ongoing off ends the pause note.
- **Cost.** Without `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` or `BRAVE_SEARCH_API_KEY` the kind fails with `source_not_configured`. A plan is about 2 US cents, a fit call about a tenth of a cent: one first look call per 100 candidates and one second look call per 30 crawled companies with someone to check. A real run for nvoke's own customers (limit 150) took 17 to 24 Brave requests and under 60 seconds.
