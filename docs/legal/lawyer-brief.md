# Brief for data protection counsel

nvoke (Danish company, CVR TODO), 13 October 2026, revised 20 October 2026 for sending. Contact: privacy@nvoke.run. Supporting documents in this folder: `lia.md`, `dpia.md`, `ropa.md`, `article-14.md`, and our desk research `docs/research/gdpr-competitors.md`.

## What nvoke does

nvoke is a paid desktop app for businesses researching other businesses. A customer searches, for example "dentists in Aarhus". Our server finds matching companies in public sources (OpenStreetMap, Brave Search API, Product Hunt, Hacker News Show HN), then crawls each company's own website: at most 8 pages (homepage, contact, about, team, impressum, privacy, legal) and 2 MB, robots.txt honoured, 1 request per second per domain, named user agent linking to an explanation page, no logins, no CAPTCHA solving. It extracts published email addresses and labels each `generic` (info@, kontakt@) or `personal` (firstname@). It checks deliverability with an MX lookup and an SMTP RCPT probe that never sends a message. The customer sees the results and can export CSV. Since 2026-10-17 it also reads the names and job titles a company publishes about its own people (team, about, imprint pages), keeps the page each came from, and builds business addresses for those people from the domain's address pattern (anna@, anna.berg@), kept only when the company's mail server accepts the address in an RCPT probe. Guesses on servers that accept any address are labeled with a low confidence. No social profiles, nothing behind a login. Question for counsel: is pattern-built addressing of named staff covered by the same B2B legitimate interest as published addresses, given the source page and the opt-out list?

Since 20 October 2026 nvoke also writes and sends email on the customer's instructions. The customer connects its own mailbox (SMTP and IMAP, an app password), picks a search or a daily drop as the source and starts a campaign once in the web app. nvoke then enrolls each new verified address, writes a short plain text sequence with Claude (through OpenRouter) from the customer's site and the recipient's public pages, sends it from the customer's mailbox in the recipient's working hours, and stops on a reply, bounce or opt-out. The customer is the sender: its name, mailbox and domain. Safeguards: no sends to Denmark or Germany (in code), verified addresses only, a fixed footer in every email saying where the address was found and how to stop, one-click unsubscribe, per-sender and global opt-outs, no tracking, 30 a day per mailbox. nvoke uses the same engine to market itself. Plan: `docs/sending/plan.md`.

## Our positions

1. Legal basis: legitimate interest, Art 6(1)(f), relying on CJEU C-621/22 (KNLTB) for the commercial interest.
2. Roles: nvoke is controller for collection, verification, the shared cache and the suppression list. Each customer is an independent controller for its own use of exported results.
3. Split cache: generic addresses are cached and shared between customers (12 months from first collection, never renewed, removed sooner if no longer on the site). Personal addresses are never shared; they live only in the finding customer's search and are deleted with it, at the latest after 12 months. Verification results are kept 30 days.
4. Suppression: hashed opt-out list (address or domain), checked before storing, serving and export, kept forever. Public opt-out page, immediate, no confirmation.
5. Transparency: public Art 14 notice (Danish and English) on our privacy page, linked from the crawler's user agent. No individual notification today.
6. Danish marketing law: Markedsføringsloven §10 requires prior consent for marketing email, including info@ and B2B. nvoke blocks every send to Denmark and Germany. Customer terms make the customer the sender, bind it to the law of each recipient's country, forbid consumer addresses and resale, and let nvoke pause any campaign. We read §10 as covering recipients in Denmark, not a Danish company's email to recipients abroad. The owner accepts that reading as a risk until counsel answers question 12.
7. Sending roles: the customer is the controller for its outreach and nvoke its processor for writing and sending. nvoke stays controller for the lead data.
8. We have treated a DPIA as required and written one. Residual risk: medium.

## Questions

1. **Art 14 for personal addresses.** Is a public notice plus opt-out sufficient for personal work addresses we hold only inside one customer's search result, given Bisnode (NSA III OSK 2538/21) and EDPB Guidelines 03/2026 Example 3? If not, must we email each person an Art 14 notice before showing the address to the customer (Art 14(3)(c))? Would such a notice email itself count as marketing under §10?
2. **Art 14 for generic addresses** that identify a person (a sole trader's kontakt@). Is relying on Art 14(5)(b) defensible for these?
3. **§10 and medvirken.** Now that nvoke sends, do the country gate, the customer terms and an in-app notice before every CSV export reduce our exposure to co-liability under the Forbrugerombudsmand's medvirken reading (vejledning 2021, chapter 12)? What else should we do (for example restrict exports, require customers to confirm a lawful basis, drop `.dk` personal addresses)?
4. **SMTP RCPT probes.** Is a deliverability probe that never sends a message processing we can base on legitimate interest? Any issue under the Danish Criminal Code or the ePrivacy rules (access to terminal equipment, data hacking provisions) or mail server terms?
5. **Controller or processor.** Is our controller position correct for personal addresses held only in one customer's results, or are we a processor for those (Dropcontact model)? Would processor status be credible, and would it move the Art 14 duty to the customer? Are we joint controllers with customers anywhere?
6. **Retention.** Are 12 months for the generic cache (from first collection), 12 months for customer search results, 30 days for verification results, and forever for opt-out hashes proportionate? Is a forever unsalted sha256 hash of an opted-out address acceptable?
7. **Sources' terms.** Is our use consistent with the OpenStreetMap ODbL and the Nominatim and Overpass usage policies (storing listing data, attribution in exports), the Brave Search API terms (storing and reselling derived results), and Product Hunt's API terms? Does a website's terms of use that forbid scraping bind us when robots.txt allows the crawl (CNIL's 2020 guidance says it does)?
8. **DPIA and Art 36.** Is our DPIA sufficient, and does anything on Datatilsynet's list of processing requiring a DPIA apply? Should we consult Datatilsynet?
9. **DPO.** Do we need to appoint one under Art 37?
10. **Customer terms.** Please review or draft the clauses: independent controller, GDPR and §10 compliance, Art 14 notice at first contact, honouring objections and passing them back, no resale, no consumer marketing, indemnity. Do we need a data sharing agreement with each customer?
11. **Other EU markets.** Customers may search Germany, Sweden or the UK. Which national e-marketing rules or notice language requirements should the terms or product address first?
12. **§10 abroad.** Does Markedsføringsloven §10 cover marketing email a Danish company (nvoke itself, or a Danish customer) sends to business recipients outside Denmark, for example in the UK or the US? If it does, what is the lawful footing for nvoke's own outreach?
13. **Processor for sending.** Is nvoke a processor when it writes and sends from the customer's mailbox on the customer's instructions, while it stays controller for the lead data it supplies? What must the Art 28 terms say, and does the AI provider (OpenRouter, Anthropic in the US) become a sub-processor of the customer?
14. **Danish co-liability for sending.** With nvoke sending, not only supplying exports, does the medvirken risk change? Is the country gate (Denmark and Germany blocked) the right set, and is it enough to block by company location and ccTLD?
15. **UK and US footing.** For B2B email to the UK (PECR, corporate subscribers) and the US (CAN-SPAM), are the footer, the one-click unsubscribe and the sender's identity enough, or is a postal address or other content needed?
16. **The notice in the email.** Does the footer line (where the address was found, how to stop) meet Art 14(3)(b) for the people emailed, together with the public notice?
